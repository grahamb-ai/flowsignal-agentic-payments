## Remediation
The represented authority-state store now serializes authority-state advancement and the final consequence boundary through the same in-process `RLock`.

`execute_protected_consequence()` performs the final authority-state comparison while holding that guard and retains the guard until represented consequence formation completes.

Authority-state advancement must acquire the same guard.

## Adversarial test
The test deliberately pauses execution after final standing resolution but before consequence formation and concurrently attempts to advance authority state.

Observed invariant:

1. final standing is resolved under the authority-state guard;
2. execution is paused before represented consequence formation;
3. a competing authority-state advancement is started;
4. the state advancement cannot commit while the final boundary remains open;
5. consequence formation completes against the standing already resolved;
6. only then may the authority-state advancement commit.

A companion test demonstrates the opposite ordering: where the authority-state change commits before the final consequence boundary, the older permit is denied with `DENIED_AUTHORITY_STATE_STALE`.

## Demonstrated scope
Within the single-process public reference harness, an authority-state change cannot commit between final standing resolution and represented consequence formation. The two operations are serialized against the same authority-state guard.

## Explicit limitation
This does **not** establish distributed atomic commit, serializability, consensus, or cross-system transactional guarantees across payment rails, databases, cloud services, message brokers, or other independently administered systems. A production implementation would require an enforcement mechanism appropriate to the actual consequence boundary, such as transactional compare-and-swap, conditional writes, database transactions, execution-token consumption at the mutating service, or an equivalent atomic control.

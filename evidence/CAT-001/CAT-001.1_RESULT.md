# CAT-001.1 — Standing at Effect / Changed Authority Before Consequence

**Qualification date:** 20 August 2026  
**Target:** `Kamanaka5502/bind-time-authority-proof` public bounded demonstrator  
**Frozen target commit:** `d71d743e23098fb58454d8d412cb4a988681d2c1`  
**Classification:** PASS — bounded published simulation mechanism; consequence formation itself NOT DEMONSTRATED

## Frozen proposition

> Authority invalidated after initial authorisation but before represented consequence formation MUST NOT result in admitted consequence.

## Target's own public claim

The frozen public documentation states that authority must still be valid at the exact moment protected consequence would bind and that when standing cannot be proven at that boundary the outcome must refuse.

## Published mechanism inspected

`bind_time_authority_fabric/advanced_execution_field.py` initializes authority as valid at epoch 1 and a standing token at epoch 1. At step 4 it mutates authority to invalid and increments the authority epoch. The decision expression is recalculated after that mutation and requires all of the following for `EXECUTE`:

- manifold condition satisfied;
- positive capacity;
- `authority.valid` true;
- standing-token signature verifies;
- token epoch equals current authority epoch.

After the step-4 revocation, both `authority.valid` and epoch equality fail, so the published decision mechanism selects `REFUSE` rather than `EXECUTE`.

A second published simulation, `adversarial_execution_field.py`, independently sets `authority_rule(...)` to false from step 4 onward and calculates `REFUSE` when authority is false.

## Result

The frozen public code contains an executable decision simulation in which authority begins valid, is changed before the later decision, and the later decision refuses rather than executes.

Therefore the narrow proposition that the **published simulation decision mechanism** does not continue to return `EXECUTE` after its represented authority revocation is supported.

**PASS — bounded published simulation mechanism.**

## Critical scope limitation

This test does **not** establish that a protected external consequence cannot form after refusal.

In `advanced_execution_field.py`, `decision` is a simulated string outcome and the commit chain appends a hash payload on every loop iteration; there is no separate protected consequence-producing operation whose non-formation is demonstrated by this file.

Likewise, the existence of a `REFUSE` decision in a simulation must not be extrapolated into universal route closure, production enforcement, external physical non-formation, or production authority isolation.

Those propositions remain separate CAT-001 proof obligations.

## Evidence discipline

This is deliberately not classified FAIL merely because the public demonstrator does not expose production enforcement. The target explicitly declares that the repository is a bounded demonstrator and not the production authority fabric.

The demonstrated claim is therefore kept narrow:

> On the frozen public simulation surface inspected for CAT-001.1, a represented authority revocation occurring after initial valid standing causes the subsequent simulated decision to be `REFUSE` rather than `EXECUTE`.

Anything stronger is NOT DEMONSTRATED by CAT-001.1.
